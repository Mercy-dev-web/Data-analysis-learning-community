# Learner Handbook

Welcome. This handbook explains how to use the learning materials and what is expected from every learner.

## 1. The purpose of the community

The purpose is to help beginners develop a disciplined, practical approach to data analysis. You are here to learn how to solve problems with data, not to collect certificates or memorise tool commands.

By the end, you should be able to:
- Understand a problem and turn it into answerable questions.
- Inspect, clean and organise data.
- Choose appropriate analytical methods.
- Use Excel, SQL, Power BI and Python for common analysis tasks.
- Check whether your results are reliable.
- Explain findings and recommendations clearly.

## 2. The non-negotiable rules

1. **Follow the sequence.** Complete the roadmap in order. Later topics rely on earlier skills.
2. **Practise before checking solutions.** Make a genuine attempt before reading a worked answer.
3. **Submit your own work.** You may learn from examples, documentation and peers, but your submitted analysis must be yours.
4. **Explain your decisions.** A formula or query without an explanation is not enough.
5. **Keep raw data unchanged.** Save a separate working copy before cleaning.
6. **Validate your results.** Check totals, row counts, data types, joins and calculations.
7. **Document assumptions.** If you make a decision because information is missing or ambiguous, write it down.
8. **Respect privacy.** Never upload private customer, student, employee, financial or otherwise confidential data.
9. **Ask specific questions.** Show what you tried, what you expected and what happened.
10. **Accept feedback and revise.** Mistakes are part of learning; refusing to correct them is not.

## 3. Weekly routine

Use this routine every week:

### Step 1 — Preview
Read the week's title, objectives and deliverable. Identify unfamiliar terms.

### Step 2 — Learn
Read the lesson carefully. Use recommended videos or documentation as support, not as a replacement for the lesson.

### Step 3 — Reproduce
Type the examples yourself. Do not only watch someone else do them. Change the example values and observe what happens.

### Step 4 — Practise independently
Complete the exercises without copying a solution. If you get stuck, write down the exact point where you are stuck.

### Step 5 — Complete the assignment
Answer the task, save your work with a clear filename and include a short explanation of your process.

### Step 6 — Review
Check your work against the rubric. Correct errors and note what you need to revisit.

### Step 7 — Record progress
Update the weekly tracker. Be honest about what you can do without assistance.

## 4. Recommended folder organisation

Keep your local work organised:

```text
data-analysis-learning/
├── raw-data/
├── working-files/
├── weekly-assignments/
│   ├── week-01/
│   ├── week-02/
│   └── ...
├── projects/
│   ├── excel-project/
│   ├── sql-project/
│   └── capstone/
└── notes/
```

Do not edit the only copy of a raw dataset. Keep source files separate from transformed files.

## 5. Assignment submission standard

Every assignment should include, where relevant:

- **Name of task:** what you are submitting.
- **Objective:** what question or skill the task addresses.
- **Dataset:** where the data came from and when it was accessed.
- **Method:** what you did and why.
- **Evidence:** workbook, query, notebook, report or screenshots.
- **Findings:** what the analysis shows.
- **Limitations:** what cannot be concluded.
- **Reflection:** what was difficult and what you learned.

Use clear file names, for example `week-04-excel-functions.xlsx` or `week-08-sql-analysis.sql`.

## 6. How to ask for help

A useful help request includes:
- The week and exercise you are working on.
- The exact question or task.
- The steps you have already tried.
- The result you expected.
- The result or error you received.
- A small sample of non-sensitive data, if necessary.

Do not post passwords, API keys, private records or confidential datasets. Remove personal information before sharing examples.

## 7. Academic honesty and responsible use of AI

AI tools can help explain concepts, suggest debugging steps and provide feedback. They should not replace your thinking.

If you use AI:
- Verify formulas, queries, code and claims.
- Do not submit generated work you cannot explain.
- Do not paste private or confidential data into public AI tools.
- Disclose substantial AI assistance when submitting a project.
- Treat AI output as a suggestion, not as evidence.

Copying a complete solution and presenting it as independent work defeats the purpose of the programme.

## 8. Data ethics and safety

- Use public, properly licensed or personally created datasets.
- Check the terms of use before downloading or redistributing data.
- Remove names, phone numbers, addresses, account details and other identifiers unless there is a clear lawful reason to use them.
- Do not attempt to identify individuals from supposedly anonymous data.
- Avoid discriminatory conclusions and unsupported claims.
- Explain sampling limitations and possible bias.
- Share only the minimum data necessary for a learning task.

## 9. Assessment and progression

The recommended pass mark is 70%. A score below 70% means you should revisit the relevant material, correct the work and resubmit. It is a learning checkpoint, not a judgement of your ability.

Milestone checks occur after:
- Week 2: foundations and data quality.
- Week 5: Excel project.
- Week 8: SQL project.
- Week 11: Power BI report.
- Week 14: Python EDA notebook.
- Week 16: capstone and portfolio.

## 10. What to do when you fall behind

Do not rush through several weeks just to appear up to date. Identify the last topic you understand, return to its exercises and rebuild confidence. Ask for help early. If necessary, extend your personal schedule while preserving the order of the curriculum.

## 11. Definition of a good analyst

A good analyst is curious, careful and honest. They do not force the data to support a preferred story. They can explain what the data says, what it does not say, how they reached a conclusion and what decision the evidence may support.
