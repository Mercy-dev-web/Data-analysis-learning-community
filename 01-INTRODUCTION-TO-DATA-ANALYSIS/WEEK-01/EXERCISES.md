# Week 1 — Practice Exercises

**Instructions:** Attempt every question before checking the answer guide. Write explanations in your own words. For scenario questions, more than one answer may be reasonable if you justify it.

## Part A — Check your understanding

1. Explain data analysis to a friend who has never worked with data.
2. What is the difference between data and information?
3. Explain why an insight is not always proof of a cause.
4. Give one example of a business decision that could be improved using data.
5. Why should an analyst understand the stakeholder's problem before opening Excel or Power BI?

## Part B — Identify the type of analysis

For each question, identify whether it is mainly descriptive, diagnostic, predictive or prescriptive. Give a one-sentence reason.

1. How many orders did a supermarket receive last month?
2. Why did orders fall in one branch compared with the previous month?
3. How many orders might the supermarket receive next December?
4. Given expected demand and limited storage, how much stock should the supermarket order?
5. Which product category contributed the most revenue last quarter?
6. Which factors may explain an increase in customer complaints?

## Part C — Metrics, KPIs and targets

A small delivery business wants to improve customer experience.

1. Suggest four metrics it could monitor.
2. Choose one metric that could be a KPI and explain why.
3. Write a measurable target for that KPI.
4. Explain why the number of social media followers may not be the best KPI for this objective.
5. Define how you would calculate the on-time delivery rate.

## Part D — Improve vague requests

Rewrite each request as a clearer analytical question. State one clarification you would ask the stakeholder.

1. “Our sales are bad. Find out what is happening.”
2. “Customers are not happy.”
3. “We need to post better content.”
4. “Students are failing.”
5. “Our expenses are too high.”

## Part E — Understand the analysis lifecycle

Put these activities in a sensible order, from the beginning of a project to the end:

- Communicate findings.
- Inspect and prepare data.
- Understand the problem.
- Review the outcome.
- Analyse the data.
- Identify and collect data.
- Define analytical questions.
- Interpret and validate results.

Then explain why validation should happen before communicating a conclusion.

## Part F — Scenario exercise

### Scenario: A campus mini-mart

A mini-mart near a university notices that its monthly revenue has decreased. The owner asks you to “check the sales and tell me what to do.”

You are not given a dataset yet.

Answer the following:

1. Who is the stakeholder?
2. What does the owner need to decide?
3. List five clarifying questions you would ask before starting.
4. Write one main analytical question.
5. Write four supporting questions.
6. Identify at least five data fields you would request.
7. Suggest three metrics that could help.
8. Identify three possible limitations.
9. Give one example of a recommendation you should NOT make before seeing the data. Explain why.

## Part G — Mini case: Instagram marketing

A small Nigerian skincare vendor says:

> “My Instagram page has 5,000 followers, but I do not make enough sales. I think my followers are not serious.”

Write a short response as the analyst. Your response should:
- avoid accepting the owner's assumption as fact,
- identify what needs to be measured,
- ask at least three clarifying questions,
- suggest the records you would need,
- explain one limitation of attributing sales to Instagram.
