# Final Assignment — Publish and Present Your Portfolio Project

## Scenario

You have completed the Week 15 capstone, **What Is Driving Retail Sales?** Your task is to prepare it so that a recruiter, mentor, classmate or business reader can understand what you did and assess the quality of your work.

This is an individual submission unless your facilitator explicitly approved a group project. If you worked with others, clearly state your own contribution.

## Part A — Polish the project (25 marks)

1. Review your Week 15 analysis across Excel, SQL, Python and Power BI.
2. Correct inconsistencies between tools and outputs.
3. Check metric definitions and labels.
4. Make sure the README explains the project and links to the relevant files.
5. Add at least three readable screenshots or visual previews with captions.
6. Include a data dictionary or link to the Week 15 data dictionary.
7. State that the dataset is synthetic and describe important limitations.

## Part B — GitHub portfolio (20 marks)

1. Improve your GitHub profile description.
2. Create or update a profile README, or submit a clear plan if you do not wish to publish one yet.
3. Make the Week 15 repository easy to navigate.
4. Pin or highlight your best work if appropriate.
5. Check all public links in a signed-out browser window.
6. Do not upload private or restricted files.

Your portfolio may be a single repository or a small set of repositories. A personal website is optional, not required.

## Part C — Presentation (25 marks)

Create either:
- A 5–7 slide presentation, or
- A 5–7 minute recorded walkthrough.

Suggested flow:
1. Title and business context.
2. Business questions and dataset.
3. Data preparation and method.
4. Key finding 1.
5. Key finding 2 and comparison.
6. Recommendations and limitations.
7. Conclusion / next steps.

You may combine slides where appropriate. Do not fill slides with paragraphs or screenshots of entire spreadsheets. Every chart should have a reason to be there.

## Part D — Career communication and reflection (20 marks)

Submit:
- One honest CV project entry (1–3 bullets).
- A 100–150 word LinkedIn project description or post.
- A 250–400 word reflection covering what you learned, a challenge, a decision you made, what you contributed and what you would improve.

Label the work as an independent, academic, guided or community project as appropriate. Do not describe it as paid employment if it was not.

## Part E — Reproducibility and professionalism (10 marks)

- Include instructions for opening or running the files.
- State software or library requirements.
- Credit data sources, tutorials, collaborators and substantial assistance.
- Check that no private data, credentials or restricted material are exposed.
- Be ready to explain the work and answer questions.

## Submission structure

You can adapt this structure:

```text
week16-portfolio-submission/
├── README.md
├── project/
│   └── link-to-your-week15-project.md
├── screenshots/
│   ├── overview.png
│   ├── detail.png
│   └── analysis.png
├── presentation/
│   └── retail-sales-presentation.pdf
├── career/
│   ├── cv-project-entry.md
│   └── linkedin-description.md
```

If your Week 15 project is already in a separate public repository, link to it rather than copying all files again.

## Marking rubric

| Area | Marks | What is assessed |
|---|---:|---|
| Project quality | 25 | Accurate, consistent analysis; clear README; readable evidence; limitations |
| GitHub portfolio | 20 | Profile/repository organisation, navigation, working links |
| Presentation | 25 | Clear story, correct metrics, useful visuals, recommendations |
| Career communication and reflection | 20 | Honest project description, specific reflection, clear writing |
| Reproducibility and professionalism | 10 | Instructions, attribution, privacy and readiness to explain |
| **Total** | **100** | **Recommended pass: 70** |

## What a strong submission looks like

A strong submission does not need expensive design or a website. It is accurate, easy to navigate, honest about its scope and clear about what the analysis means. The learner can explain the work without relying on a script or claiming results that the data cannot support.
