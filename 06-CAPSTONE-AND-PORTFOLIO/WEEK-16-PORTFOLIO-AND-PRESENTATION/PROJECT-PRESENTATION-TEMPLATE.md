# Project Presentation Template

**Format:** 5–7 slides or a 5–7 minute walkthrough.

The audience may not know SQL, pandas, DAX or Power Query. Explain the business meaning first and mention technical details only when they help.

## Slide 1 — Title and context

Include:
- Project title.
- Your name.
- Project type (independent, academic, guided or community).
- One sentence describing the business situation.

Suggested opening:
“This project examines sales performance for a fictional Nigerian skincare and personal-care retailer over six months.”

## Slide 2 — Questions and dataset

Include:
- Two or three business questions.
- Dataset source and period.
- Number of rows and what one row represents.
- Important fields.

Mention that the Week 15 dataset is synthetic.

## Slide 3 — Preparation and method

Show the main steps, not every click:
- Data-quality checks.
- Important cleaning or transformations.
- Metrics calculated.
- Tools used and what each contributed.

For example: SQL for structured queries, Python for cleaning and grouped analysis, Excel for checks and summaries, Power BI for the interactive report—only if you actually used each tool.

## Slide 4 — Finding one

Include:
- One chart or clear comparison.
- A descriptive title.
- One verified number with units.
- A sentence explaining the business meaning.

## Slide 5 — Finding two

Include:
- Another chart or comparison.
- What changed across products, cities, channels or months.
- Why a decision-maker may care.

Do not repeat slide 4 in a different chart type.

## Slide 6 — Recommendations and limitations

Include:
- Two practical recommendations.
- What management should verify before acting.
- Important limitations of the dataset and analysis.

For this capstone, remember that gross profit subtracts product cost only; it is not net profit. The data is synthetic and cannot prove why a trend occurred.

## Slide 7 — Conclusion and next steps

Summarise:
- The main answer to the business question.
- The most useful next action or further analysis.
- A brief thank-you / question prompt.

## Speaker notes

For each slide, write:
- What the audience is looking at.
- What the key message is.
- What evidence supports it.
- What you want the audience to remember.

## Presentation review

Before presenting:
- Check every figure against the source analysis.
- Use readable font sizes.
- Label currency, dates and percentages.
- Avoid unexplained acronyms and jargon.
- Do not read entire paragraphs from slides.
- Rehearse within the time limit.
- Prepare to explain one limitation and one analytical decision.
