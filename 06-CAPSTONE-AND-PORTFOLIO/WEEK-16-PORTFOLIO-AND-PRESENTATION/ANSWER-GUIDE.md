# Answer Guide and Model Examples

These are examples, not text to copy without checking. Your wording and findings must match your own analysis.

## Exercise 3 — Finding, recommendation or assumption?

1. “Net revenue was higher in June than in January in the dataset.” — **Finding**, if your calculated monthly results confirm it.
2. “The June increase happened because the company ran a successful advert.” — **Unsupported assumption.** The dataset does not contain campaign exposure or attribution evidence.
3. “Management should check campaign records before attributing the increase to advertising.” — **Recommendation.**
4. “Body Lotion had the highest net revenue among the products in this sample.” — **Finding**, if confirmed by your results.
5. “Customers in Lagos are more loyal than customers in Abuja.” — **Unsupported assumption.** Repeat purchase behaviour, customer history and a defined loyalty measure would be needed.

Possible careful rewrite:
“Did campaign activity contribute to the June change? This cannot be tested with the current dataset. Management could compare campaign dates, spend and channel-level outcomes with a suitable baseline.”

## Exercise 4 — Stronger project bullet

Example:
“Analysed a six-month synthetic retail order dataset using Excel, MySQL, Python and Power BI; calculated net revenue and gross profit, compared performance by month, product, city and channel, and built an interactive report to communicate findings and follow-up questions.”

This describes work performed without inventing a percentage increase or claiming the business adopted the recommendations.

## Exercise 5 — Example responses

**What does one row represent?**  
“One row represents one order line in this capstone dataset. The supplied sample has one product line per Order_ID, but real retail orders can contain multiple product lines.”

**How did you calculate gross profit?**  
“For each row, I calculated net revenue as quantity multiplied by unit price, less the discount. I then subtracted product cost, calculated as quantity multiplied by unit cost. This is gross profit before operating expenses, not net profit.”

**What is one limitation?**  
“The data is synthetic and covers only six months. It is useful for practising analysis but cannot establish market-wide patterns or explain the causes of changes.”

## README quality checklist

A strong README answers these questions:
- What is this project about?
- Why does the question matter?
- What does the dataset represent?
- What steps did you take?
- What are the main findings?
- What do you recommend?
- What are the limitations?
- Where are the files and how can they be opened?

## Presentation model

Do not place every result on the slides. Choose two or three findings that answer the business question. For each finding:
1. State the question.
2. Show one relevant visual or comparison.
3. State the verified result.
4. Explain why it matters.
5. State what should be checked next.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Calling gross profit “net profit”.
- Describing revenue as profit.
- Claiming an advertising campaign caused a change without campaign data.
- Claiming customers are loyal based only on revenue totals.
- Publishing a PBIX or notebook without explaining how to open it.
- Copying a tutorial project and presenting every part as original.
- Adding a made-up business impact to a CV.
- Using unreadable screenshots or charts with missing units.
- Listing tools you did not actually use.

## Self-review before submission

Ask a classmate to choose one finding and trace it back to the relevant workbook, query, notebook or dashboard. If they cannot find the supporting evidence, improve the README or your analysis documentation.
